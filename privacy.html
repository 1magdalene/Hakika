Privacy Policy

A plain-language explanation of what Hakika collects, why, and who can see it.

Last updated: 26 July 2026

Hakika is a personal project, built to help caregivers create a simple, physical safety card for someone in their care — an elder living with dementia, a child on the autism spectrum, or anyone else who may wander and struggle to communicate who they are. This page explains what information the site collects, how it's used, and who is able to see it.

This is a small, independently-built project, not a registered company or a government service — there is no legal team behind this policy. It's written in plain language to be honest about exactly what the site does, rather than as a formal legal document. If you're a caregiver with questions about your own or your elder's data, reach out using the contact details at the bottom of this page.

What information is collected

When someone registers on Hakika, the following is collected:

  • About the caregiver: full name, phone number, relationship to the person in their care, a chosen username, and a password.
  • About the person being registered: their full name, a brief condition summary (e.g. "early-stage dementia"), their general home area, and one short safe instruction for a stranger who finds them.

Hakika deliberately does not ask for exact home addresses, national ID numbers, full medical histories, or financial information — none of these are collected at any point in the registration form.

How your password is handled

Your password is never stored as plain, readable text. Before it ever touches the database, it's run through a one-way scrambling process (bcrypt password hashing) — the result can be checked against on future logins, but can't be reversed back into your original password, even by someone with direct access to the database.

Who can see what

Every profile is split into two tiers, and this split is enforced by how the system is built, not left to chance:

  • Public tier (visible to anyone who reads or scans the printed card): the elder or child's name, the condition summary, the one safe instruction, and the caregiver's phone number. This is deliberately the minimum needed for a stranger to help.
  • Caregiver tier (visible only after logging in): the same information, plus the general home area.
  • Never shown anywhere: exact home addresses, ID numbers, or financial details — these are never collected in the first place, so there's nothing of the kind to expose.

Where information is stored

Registered accounts and profiles are stored in a MongoDB database. The site itself is hosted on Vercel. Fonts used on this site are loaded from Google Fonts, which means visiting any page on this site makes a request to Google's servers to load those fonts — no personal registration data is sent to Google as part of that request.

How you stay logged in

After registering or logging in, a signed login token is stored in your own browser (in what's called localStorage) — this is what keeps you logged in between visits. It stays on your device, expires after 7 days, and is never shared with anyone else.

What Hakika does not do

  • Hakika does not sell, rent, or share your information with advertisers or other third parties.
  • Hakika does not run ads, and does not use third-party analytics or tracking scripts.
  • Hakika does not use your information for anything beyond operating the card/profile system described above.

Children and people who can't consent for themselves

Hakika profiles for children or elders who cannot give their own informed consent are created and managed by an adult caregiver on their behalf. If you're registering a profile for someone in your care, you're confirming that you have the right and the responsibility to make that decision for them.

Changing or deleting your information

You can update your profile's details at any time by logging back in. If you'd like your account and all associated data permanently deleted, contact the email address below — as this is currently a small, personally-run project rather than an automated service, deletion requests are handled manually.

Changes to this policy

If what Hakika collects or how it's used changes, this page will be updated and the "last updated" date at the top will change accordingly.
